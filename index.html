Key findings

  1. Parents bring most of the volume. 4,239 of the 4,848 Mitra sessions are from parents, and 79.5% of them finish.
  2. Youth drop out the most. Only 47.8% finish. The most common stop point is right after Age.
  3. Answer quality is best for Educators and weakest for Parents. In Mitra, 84.6% of educators answers are clear or rich, against 54.3% for parents.
  4. Many parent answers are vague. 40.6% are on topic but say little, for example "good life" or "good education".
  5. Some parent sessions look copied. 183 parent sessions (5.7%) gave the same answers, word for word, as another session. They are mostly from Varanasi, Rohtas, Sitamarhi. No copying was found for Youth, Educators or General Public. See section F in the Parents tab.
  6. Mitra's follow-up question helps. When Mitra asks one, the answer improves 30.1% of the time for parents. Clear or rich parent answers rise from 38.6% to 54.3%.
  7. Weakest question: Youth, "Confidence". Only 7.8% of answers are clear or rich, and 20.8% are no response or off-topic.
  8. Youth completion is adjusted. 63 youth sessions are marked 'Completed' but only have an age. They look like age screen-outs, so they are not counted as finished.
  9. Some profile answers were not recorded. 628 parents and 80 youth have no state recorded, yet they went on to answer later questions. This looks like a Mitra capture gap, not people leaving. These people are counted as continuing, not as drop-offs.

How answers were rated

Every answer was read and rated against the question it was answering. Length alone did not decide the rating.

  • No response: skip, nothing, don't know, refusal, "want to submit", gibberish or filler such as "ok".
  • Off-topic: understandable, but it does not answer the question that was asked.
  • Vague: on topic, but generic with no real content, for example "good life" or "good education".
  • Clear: on topic, with a concrete point such as a named job, specific skills or a specific change. A short answer like "Doctor" counts when the question asks for something short.
  • Rich: specific, and it also gives a reason, an example or several well-made points.
  • "After follow-up" rates the first answer together with the reply to Mitra's follow-up question.
Data notes and limits
  • Youth: no answers at all in the V2 export for: What one thing would you change about your school or college? (left out of the funnel).
  • Youth: 63 sessions are marked Completed but have only Age (ages 8, 9, 10, 11, 26, 27, 28, 29, 30, 32, 33, 34, 35, 38, 40, 44) - these look like age screen-outs, not real completions.
  • Educators: no answers at all in the V2 export for: Do schools build these skills in children today? (left out of the funnel).
  • Files used: for each group, the Main and Others tables were combined into one consolidated dataset before analysis (V2 only; the Youth V1 files were left out).
  • Parent answer quality is rated on a random 1,000 parents. All other groups, and every Google Form row, are rated in full.
  • Identical answers were rated once and the rating was applied to every copy. All answers were rated.
  • The ratings were made by AI readers following the rubric above, and samples were spot-checked. Treat small differences of 2-3 points as noise.
  • Google Form groups are small (Parents 22, General Public 19, Youth 43), so treat those comparisons as indicative.
  • Drop-off is measured on Mitra only, because a Google Form records only submitted responses.
